A little guide

How to use

△

01 / Gum Bichromate

Make a print with light

  1. Get watercolor paper.
  2. Mix gum arabic solution with watercolor paint.
  3. Add potassium bichromate. The mixture is now light-sensitive; a slightly dark room is enough.
  4. Brush the paper and let it dry for about two hours. Add another layer and dry again if you like.
  5. Load your image; convert it to black & white or negative if needed.
  6. Set the exposure (usually one to two hours), the black-screen time, and the image size. Adjust projector focus and sharpness.
  7. Put dried paper in a clip frame in a dark place. Start Display negative and position the screen or projector.
  8. After exposure, soak the paper image-down in water for about 20 minutes, checking periodically. Remove and dry when ready.

02 / Photo Paper

Work under a red safelight

  1. Prepare photosensitive paper, developer, stop bath, and fixer.
  2. Work under a red safelight. Load an image, convert it to black & white and negative if desired, and adjust its size.
  3. Turn on Safety mode before any display. The screen and image will use dim red light.
  4. Use device brightness only for pre- or post-exposure setup, never during an exposure.
  5. Set pre-display black, display duration, and post-display black. Run a strip or multi-image test to find a suitable duration.
  6. Start Display negative and position the screen or projector over the paper.
  7. Develop, stop, and fix using the same processing times as your test, then dry.

Strip test reveals vertical bands from left to right: the first receives the longest exposure, and the last the shortest. Multi-image copies receive increasing exposure times; each copy turns black when its time ends.

Five quick taps or clicks on the display end playback. Settings stay in this browser; photos are never uploaded. Wake Lock requires a supported browser and a secure context.
